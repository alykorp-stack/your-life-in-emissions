The whole record

We have yearly global emissions of carbon dioxide (CO2) and methane (CH4) back to 1850. The lines show how much of everything emitted since 1850 had been released by each year. The shaded band is your lifetime.

md`**${pct(lifeShare.co2)}** of all CO2 and **${pct(lifeShare.ch4)}** of all CH4 recorded since 1850 was released while you have been alive. Half of all CO2 had been emitted by **${half.co2}** (${agePhrase(half.co2)}), half of all CH4 by **${half.ch4}** (${agePhrase(half.ch4)}).`

The steep part of the curve is recent. Dots mark the halfway years, and the ticks inside your band are your ages. Click the chart to try another birth year.

What the world was going through

Emissions are not a smooth climb. Wars, recessions, oil shocks and the pandemic bent the curve. For each event we compare emissions with the trend of the ten years before it (the dashed line) and show the three that moved CO2 furthest from that trend.

measured = events.filter(e => e.kind === "event" && e.gas === "CO2" && e.start > birth)
bigThree = measured.slice().sort((a, b) => Math.abs(b.pct_vs_trend) - Math.abs(a.pct_vs_trend)).slice(0, 3).sort((a, b) => a.start - b.start)
signedPct = v => (v > 0 ? "+" : "\u2212") + Math.abs(v) + "%"
md`${measured.length === 0 ? "None of the events in our list fall inside your lifetime." : `**${measured.length}** events in our list fall inside your lifetime. The ${bigThree.length === 3 ? "three" : ""} that moved CO2 furthest from its trend: ${bigThree.map(e => `**${e.event}** (${signedPct(e.pct_vs_trend)})`).join(", ")}.`}`

What is the dashed line? It is where emissions were heading before the event. We take the ten years just before it, measure how fast CO2 was growing in a typical year in that stretch, and keep that same pace going through the event’s years. Think of it as “business as usual”: a reference line, not a forecast.

How to read the percentage. Add up all the CO2 emitted during the event and compare it with what the dashed line would have given over the same years. A minus means emissions came in below the earlier pace; a plus means above it.

example = bigThree.length ? bigThree.slice().sort((a, b) => Math.abs(b.pct_vs_trend) - Math.abs(a.pct_vs_trend))[0] : null
exYears = example ? example.end - example.start + 1 : 0
md`${example ? `**An example from your lifetime: ${example.event}** (${example.start === example.end ? example.start : example.start + " to " + example.end}). In the ten years before it, CO2 grew by about **${example.trend_growth_pct.toFixed(1)}% a year**. Keeping that pace, the world would have emitted about **${Math.round(example.baseline_mt / 1000)} Gt** over ${exYears === 1 ? "that year" : "those " + exYears + " years"}. It actually emitted **${Math.round(example.actual_mt / 1000)} Gt**, which is **${Math.abs(example.pct_vs_trend)}% ${example.pct_vs_trend > 0 ? "more" : "less"}** than the dashed line.` : ""}`

This shows how emissions moved while an event was going on. It does not prove the event caused it. Hover a year on the chart for the numbers behind it.

What if emissions had stopped growing?

So far we have described what happened. Now a thought experiment: hold emissions at the level of your birth year, and compare. The shaded gap is how much more the world emitted than it would have.

flat = w.filter(r => r.year >= birth)
gapGt = rate => d3.sum(flat, r => r.co2 - flat[0].co2 * Math.pow(1 + rate / 100, r.year - birth)) / 1000
nowGt = flat[flat.length - 1].co2 / 1000
path = rate => rate === 0 ? `stayed at its ${birth} level` : `${rate < 0 ? "fallen" : "grown"} ${Math.abs(rate)}% a year from ${birth}`
md`If CO2 had ${path(sliderRate)}, the world would have emitted **${Math.round(Math.abs(gapGt(sliderRate)))} Gt** ${gapGt(sliderRate) >= 0 ? "less" : "more"} CO2 during your lifetime.`
md`**What does that mean in everyday terms?** Right now the world emits about **${nowGt.toFixed(0)} Gt** of CO2 every year. A gap of ${Math.round(Math.abs(gapGt(sliderRate)))} Gt is the amount the world emits in about **${(Math.abs(gapGt(sliderRate)) / nowGt).toFixed(1)} years** at today's rate. It is only a way to feel how big the number is.`

Use the slider to test other paths, from emissions falling 5% a year to rising 3%; the numbers above follow it. This is an illustration of scale, not a forecast: real emissions move with population and the economy.

How this was made, and what to be careful about

  • Data. Annual CO2 and CH4 emissions by country, 1850 to 2024, excluding land use: PRIMAP-hist v2.2 (to 2018), Global Carbon Budget and EDGAR (later years). CH4 is converted to CO2 equivalent at GWP100 = 27.9. World totals are the sum of countries.
  • Borders. History is assigned to today’s borders, so emissions of the Soviet Union appear under Russia and its other successor states.
  • Events. “Above or below trend” compares an event’s years with a constant-growth trend fitted to the ten years before it. A baseline can contain an earlier shock (the trend used for 2022 includes the pandemic), and policy milestones such as Kyoto and Paris get no number because a trend cannot measure their effect.
  • Per person. The default per person ranking leaves out countries with under one million people, which would otherwise top any list. You can add any country yourself. Kuwait’s 1991 spike is the Gulf War oil-well fires.
  • The thought experiment is a simple calculation, not a model of what was possible.
  • Gaps. A few small territories lack some years; countries missing a value count as zero in the totals.
